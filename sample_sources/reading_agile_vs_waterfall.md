# Agile vs. Waterfall Project Management

Engineering managers choose a delivery approach based on how well the requirements are understood and how costly change is.

## Waterfall

Waterfall runs phases in sequence: requirements, design, implementation, verification and maintenance. Each phase ends with a gate review. It suits projects with stable requirements and high cost of change, such as hardware with long tooling lead times. The risk is that problems surface late, during verification.

## Agile and Scrum

Agile delivers working increments in short iterations called sprints, usually two weeks. Scrum defines three roles: the product owner, who orders the backlog by value; the scrum master, who removes impediments; and the development team. Daily stand-ups, sprint reviews and retrospectives create fast feedback. Like kaizen in lean operations, retrospectives drive continuous improvement.

## Kanban for knowledge work

Kanban boards borrow the pull system from lean manufacturing. Work-in-progress limits on each column expose the bottleneck, echoing the theory of constraints. Little's Law applies: lower WIP gives shorter lead time at the same throughput.

## Hybrid approaches

Hardware-software products often use a hybrid: stage-gate reviews for the physical design and agile sprints for firmware and software. The engineering manager's job is to align the cadences at integration milestones.
